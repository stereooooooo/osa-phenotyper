# Scientific rationale primary-source review

**Scope:** Adult OSA diagnostic testing and home sleep apnea test (HSAT) adequacy; Inspire unilateral hypoglossal nerve stimulation (HGNS) evaluation, labeling, response context, and follow-up.

**Audience:** Clinicians, scientific reviewers, and clinical or industry collaborators.

**Literature and regulatory search completed:** 2026-08-13.

**Status:** Research input for `docs/scientific-rationale-and-decision-logic.md`. This note does not itself authorize a change to Precision Sleep clinical logic. Any change must pass the repository's clinical-change gate and clinician review.

## Executive conclusions

1. Precision Sleep should use questionnaires and clinical findings to decide **which objective diagnostic pathway needs consideration**, never to diagnose OSA without PSG or HSAT.
2. For an uncomplicated adult at increased risk of moderate-to-severe OSA, either PSG or a technically adequate HSAT is guideline supported. If a single HSAT is negative, inconclusive, or technically inadequate, AASM strongly recommends PSG rather than another HSAT.
3. AASM defines a technically adequate conventional HSAT as at least four hours of adequate flow and oximetry during an attempt encompassing the habitual sleep period. That is a minimum technical boundary, not proof that REM, position, central breathing, hypoventilation, or every decision-relevant feature was adequately sampled.
4. PSG is preferred when important comorbidity or a competing sleep disorder makes HSAT less reliable or incomplete, including significant cardiorespiratory disease, possible respiratory muscle weakness, awake or suspected sleep-related hypoventilation, chronic opioid use, prior stroke, or severe insomnia.
5. Current U.S. Inspire labeling is device specific. It covers moderate-to-severe OSA with AHI 15-100, prior PAP failure or intolerance, no palatal complete concentric collapse (CCC), and no more than 25% central plus mixed events. BMI above 40 is a warning/data boundary, not a listed universal contraindication.
6. Medicare and other payer requirements are separate from FDA labeling. A current Medicare local coverage determination uses narrower criteria, including age at least 22, BMI below 35, PSG AHI 15-65 within 24 months, and explicit DISE documentation. Precision Sleep should show the applicable payer rules without presenting them as universal clinical or device rules.
7. DISE is essential to the unilateral Inspire pathway because palatal CCC must be excluded. Other DISE patterns, demographics, BMI, PAP pressure, and research endotypes can inform counseling, but the current evidence does not support a validated individual response score or probability.
8. Objective sleep testing is recommended after a non-PAP intervention. Either PSG or HSAT can be appropriate after HGNS, selected according to the clinical question; a small randomized pathway trial does not establish one universal follow-up protocol.

## Evidence hierarchy used in this note

| Category | What it controls | What it must not be confused with |
|---|---|---|
| **FDA device labeling and SSED** | The U.S. indication, contraindications, warnings, and evidentiary basis for the specific Inspire system | A guideline recommendation, payer coverage policy, or prediction of response |
| **Professional guideline or official statement** | Recommended diagnostic or management pathway based on evidence review and expert judgment | Device labeling or guaranteed coverage |
| **Payer coverage policy** | Documentation and selection conditions for payment in a defined jurisdiction and effective period | A universal device contraindication or universal standard of care |
| **Original clinical study** | Estimated efficacy, harms, associations, and uncertainty in the studied population | A validated individual prediction rule unless externally validated for that use |
| **Local governance** | A deliberately conservative workflow or referral boundary chosen by Capital ENT/Precision Sleep | A claim that every clinician, device, or payer uses the same boundary |

## 1. Diagnostic testing and HSAT adequacy

### 1.1 What the AASM guideline establishes

**Primary source:** Kapur VK, Auckley DH, Chowdhuri S, et al. Clinical Practice Guideline for Diagnostic Testing for Adult Obstructive Sleep Apnea. *J Clin Sleep Med.* 2017;13(3):479-504. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.6506) | [Full guideline](https://aasm.org/resources/clinicalguidelines/diagnostic-testing-osa.pdf) | [PubMed/PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5337595/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

The guideline makes these decision-relevant statements:

- Diagnostic testing should occur with a comprehensive sleep evaluation and adequate follow-up. PSG is the standard diagnostic test when the clinical evaluation raises concern for OSA.
- Questionnaires, clinical tools, and prediction algorithms must not diagnose OSA without PSG or HSAT (strong recommendation).
- PSG or a technically adequate HSAT may diagnose OSA in an **uncomplicated** adult with signs and symptoms indicating increased risk of moderate-to-severe OSA (strong recommendation).
- If one HSAT is negative, inconclusive, or technically inadequate, PSG should be performed (strong recommendation). The guideline specifically advises against simply repeating HSAT in this situation because a second test is more likely to remain negative or inadequate and can delay a definitive diagnosis.
- PSG, rather than HSAT, is recommended for significant cardiorespiratory disease, potential respiratory muscle weakness from neuromuscular disease, awake hypoventilation or suspected sleep-related hypoventilation, chronic opioid use, prior stroke, or severe insomnia (strong recommendation).
- When an initial PSG is negative but suspicion remains, a second PSG may be considered (weak recommendation).

**Technical minimum:** The guideline defines a technically adequate HSAT as at least four hours of adequate oximetry and flow data during a recording attempt that encompasses the habitual sleep period. It describes technically adequate devices as conventional flow/effort/oximetry configurations or peripheral arterial tonometry (PAT) with oximetry and actigraphy.

**Limitations and interpretation:**

- The evidence supporting the four-hour boundary is indirect. The guideline explicitly reports no direct evidence that fewer than four hours worsens clinical outcomes.
- Four hours of signal is therefore a minimum acquisition criterion, not a complete adequacy score. A recording may meet it yet inadequately sample REM sleep, non-supine sleep, a suspected central-breathing problem, or another clinically important state.
- “Significant” cardiorespiratory disease and “severe” insomnia require clinician judgment. Precision Sleep should not silently equate any cardiovascular diagnosis or an isolated questionnaire score with these guideline categories.
- HSAT is less sensitive than PSG. A negative result must be interpreted in the context of pretest probability and the broader clinical evaluation.

### 1.2 Arousal-based disease can be missed by most HSAT devices

**Primary authoritative source:** Malhotra RK, Kirsch DB, Kristo DA, et al. Polysomnography for Obstructive Sleep Apnea Should Include Arousal-Based Scoring: An AASM Position Statement. *J Clin Sleep Med.* 2018;14(7):1245-1247. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.7234) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6040795/) | [AASM statement](https://aasm.org/advocacy/position-statements/polysomnography-for-obstructive-sleep-apnea-should-include-arousal-based-scoring-an-american-academy-of-sleep-medicine-position-statement/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- AASM states that PSG should include arousal-based hypopnea scoring and/or an RDI that includes respiratory effort-related arousals when clinically relevant.
- Because most HSAT devices do not record EEG arousals, AASM states that PSG should be performed when a patient at increased risk for OSA has a negative HSAT.
- This supports escalation to PSG for persistent sleepiness, fatigue, insomnia, or neurocognitive symptoms despite a negative HSAT. It does **not** allow Precision Sleep to diagnose upper-airway resistance syndrome from symptoms alone.

### 1.3 Automated HSAT output is not sufficient by itself

**Primary authoritative source:** Rosen IM, Kirsch DB, Carden KA, et al. Clinical Use of a Home Sleep Apnea Test: An Updated AASM Position Statement. *J Clin Sleep Med.* 2018;14(12):2075-2077. [AASM statement](https://aasm.org/advocacy/position-statements/clinical-use-of-a-home-sleep-apnea-test-an-updated-american-academy-of-sleep-medicine-position-statement/) | [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30518456/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- HSAT is an alternative to PSG for selected uncomplicated adults, not population screening.
- The test must be ordered following a medical evaluation.
- Diagnosis, efficacy assessment, and treatment decisions should not rely solely on automatically scored HSAT data; raw data require qualified physician review and interpretation.

**Precision Sleep implication:** Importing numbers from a report can organize the chart and raise quality flags. It cannot replace review of the signed interpretation and, when decision-relevant uncertainty exists, review of the underlying recording.

### 1.4 Device-derived sleep stages and sampling uncertainty

**Original prospective validation:** Zhang Z, Sowho M, Otvos T, et al. A comparison of automated and manual sleep staging and respiratory event recognition in a portable sleep diagnostic device with in-lab sleep study. *J Clin Sleep Med.* 2020;16(4):563-573. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.8278) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7161441/) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32022670/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- In a prospective simultaneous WatchPAT/PSG program (262 total; 170 in the independent validation set), WatchPAT sleep time and REM time were algorithmic estimates. Manual review improved agreement with PSG, especially for REM time and categorical AHI agreement.
- The authors reported only moderate correlation for automated REM time versus PSG, improved with manual editing.

**Limitations:** Single technology and an algorithm-development/validation setting; findings do not establish a universal REM-minute cutoff for every HSAT or clinical decision.

**Original multi-night PAT study:** Tschopp S, Wimmer W, Caversaccio M, Borner U, Tschopp K. Night-to-night variability in obstructive sleep apnea using peripheral arterial tonometry: a case for multiple night testing. *J Clin Sleep Med.* 2021;17(9):1751-1758. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.9300) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8636340/) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33783347/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- In 51 patients tested for three nights, 35% varied by at least 10 pAHI events/hour and approximately one quarter were assigned a different severity category by a single night compared with the three-night average. Supine time explained part of the variability.

**Limitations:** Small, single-center PAT study; it supports uncertainty awareness but does not supersede the AASM recommendation to use PSG after a negative/inadequate HSAT and does not establish routine multi-night testing for everyone.

### 1.5 Decision rules justified by these sources

| Clinical state | Evidence-supported Precision Sleep action | Boundary that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| No objective sleep study | Recommend clinician-selected PSG or HSAT based on the comprehensive evaluation | Questionnaires can estimate risk, not diagnose OSA |
| Uncomplicated adult, increased risk of moderate-to-severe OSA | Either technically adequate HSAT or PSG is reasonable | Patient preference, access, and clinician judgment still matter |
| Negative, inconclusive, or technically inadequate HSAT with ongoing suspicion | Promote PSG as the next diagnostic test | Do not automatically repeat HSAT or close the diagnosis |
| Significant cardiorespiratory disease, neuromuscular respiratory weakness, suspected hypoventilation, chronic opioids, prior stroke, or severe insomnia | Promote PSG | These categories require clinical verification; checkbox presence alone should not finalize the route |
| Persistent symptoms with negative HSAT or concern for arousal-based disease | Promote PSG with arousal-based scoring | Do not diagnose from symptoms or an HSAT RDI surrogate alone |
| Adequate positive HSAT with mild/moderate result near a decision boundary | Flag uncertainty and let the clinician decide whether PSG would materially change care | Do not automatically invalidate the HSAT or mandate PSG |
| Low REM, limited position sampling, or short estimated sleep time | Qualify subtype/severity confidence and expose the missing sample | AASM provides no universal REM-minute or position-duration threshold for every decision |

## 2. Unilateral Inspire HGNS

### 2.1 Universal device-specific FDA labeling

**Primary regulatory sources:**

- U.S. FDA. Inspire Upper Airway Stimulation, PMA P130008/S090, Summary of Safety and Effectiveness Data (SSED), approved 2023-06-08. [FDA SSED](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf13/P130008S090b.pdf). Accessed 2026-08-13.
- U.S. FDA. Inspire System Implant Manual, Models 3028, 4063, 4340, labeling associated with P130008/S090. [FDA labeling](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf13/P130008S090C.pdf). Accessed 2026-08-13.
- U.S. FDA PMA database entry for P130008 and supplements. [PMA database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpma/pma.cfm?PMANumber=P130008&SupplementNumber=&start_search=1). Accessed 2026-08-13.

For the standard adult/adolescent unilateral Inspire pathway, the S090 labeling establishes:

- Moderate-to-severe OSA with AHI 15-100.
- Adults age 22 or older and a separate indication for ages 18-21.
- Confirmed PAP failure or intolerance.
- Absence of complete concentric collapse at the soft palate.
- Central plus mixed apneas greater than 25% of total AHI are a contraindication.

The label defines:

- **PAP failure:** inability to eliminate OSA, with AHI greater than 15 despite PAP use.
- **PAP intolerance:** inability to sustain the label's use threshold (more than four hours per night on more than five nights per week) or unwillingness to continue after an attempt, for example returning the PAP system.

The implant manual lists BMI above 40 under warnings: a significant number of patients above BMI 40 had not been studied and safety/effectiveness were unknown. **BMI above 40 is not listed in the FDA contraindications.**

**Important labeling limitations:**

- The 2023 expansion to AHI up to 100 and BMI up to 40 relied on retrospective ADHERE registry evidence, not a new randomized trial. FDA required a prospective five-year post-approval study in the newly expanded AHI/BMI populations.
- The BMI 35-40 outcome subgroup in the labeling was small (for example, 46 patients in the responder analysis), so absence of a significant between-group difference does not prove equivalent individual response at all BMIs.
- Labeling is device specific and may change. Precision Sleep should store the device, labeling version, and review date rather than use one generic “HGNS eligible” rule for all systems.

### 2.2 What DISE establishes, and what it does not

**Original selection study:** Vanderveken OM, Maurer JT, Hohenhorst W, et al. Evaluation of Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy as a Patient Selection Tool for Implanted Upper Airway Stimulation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea. *J Clin Sleep Med.* 2013;9(5):433-438. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.2658) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3629316/) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23674933/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- Among 21 implanted patients, the 16 without palatal CCC had AHI improve from 37.6 to 11.1 events/hour; the small CCC group had poor outcomes. This early study informed exclusion of palatal CCC from later unilateral Inspire trials and labeling.
- **Limitation:** only five patients had CCC. This is sufficient to explain the conservative device boundary, but not to estimate an individual's probability of response from DISE.

**Pivotal study:** Strollo PJ Jr, Soose RJ, Maurer JT, et al. Upper-Airway Stimulation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea. *N Engl J Med.* 2014;370:139-149. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1308659) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24401051/) | [Journal](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1308659). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- STAR was a prospective, multicenter, single-group cohort of 126 implanted PAP-intolerant patients, with a randomized withdrawal experiment among selected responders.
- At 12 months, median AHI fell 68%, from 29.3 to 9.0, and ODI fell 70%, from 25.4 to 7.4. Procedure-related serious adverse events were below 2%.
- The trial excluded palatal CCC, clinically important central/mixed disease, BMI above 32, and major compromising anatomy. Participants were 83% men with mean BMI 28.4.
- **Limitation:** the main effectiveness cohort lacked a parallel untreated control; the withdrawal phase included responders, and the trial was manufacturer funded. Its outcomes do not create an individual response guarantee, especially outside the studied phenotype.

**Multicenter response-context study:** Huyett P, Kent DT, D'Agostino MA, et al. Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy and Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation Outcomes: A Multicenter Cohort Study. *Laryngoscope.* 2021;131:1676-1682. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/lary.29396) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8362605/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- Retrospective cohort of 343 implanted adults at 10 academic centers with blinded DISE review.
- Complete oropharyngeal lateral-wall obstruction was associated with lower response than partial/none (58% vs 74%); complete tongue obstruction was associated with higher response than partial/none (78% vs 68%).
- **Limitations:** retrospective observational analysis, selected implanted population, multiple DISE comparisons, and outcome based largely on titration PSG. It supports directional counseling context only. It does not justify treating partial collapse as equivalent to complete collapse or calculating an individual success tier.

**DISE interpretation reliability:** Gillespie MB, Reddy RP, White DR, et al. A trial of drug-induced sleep endoscopy in the surgical management of sleep-disordered breathing. *Laryngoscope.* 2013;123:277-282. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/lary.23506) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22952110/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- In a 38-patient prospective study, DISE changed the proposed surgical plan in 62%; blinded scoring had good, not perfect, interrater agreement (kappa 0.65).
- This supports trained clinician interpretation and cautions against treating DISE as a perfectly reproducible numeric test.

### 2.3 Response associations must remain separate from candidacy

**Prospective observational registry:** Thaler E, Schwab R, Maurer J, et al. Results of the ADHERE upper airway stimulation registry and predictors of therapy efficacy. *Laryngoscope.* 2020;130:1333-1338. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/lary.28286) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31520484/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- Of 1,017 enrolled patients, 382 had completed 12-month follow-up in the reported analysis. Median AHI decreased from 32.8 to 9.5, mean use was 5.6 hours/night, and lower BMI plus female sex remained response-associated in the multivariable model.
- **Limitations:** observational registry, incomplete 12-month follow-up, post hoc predictor analysis, selection under clinical eligibility rules, and manufacturer involvement. These associations do not establish a calibrated individual probability.

**Later registry analysis:** Suurna MV, Steffen A, Boon M, et al. Impact of Body Mass Index and Discomfort on Upper Airway Stimulation: ADHERE Registry 2020 Update. *Laryngoscope.* 2021;131:2616-2624. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/lary.29755) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34626128/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- AHI and sleepiness improved across BMI groups, but the reported surgical-response proportion was lower in the BMI 32-35 group than the BMI-at-most-32 group (59.8% vs 72.2%); use was also modestly lower.
- **Limitations:** observational, nonrandomized BMI comparison and one-year follow-up; this is response context, not a contraindication.

**Precision Sleep rule:** AHI, central/mixed percentage, PAP failure/intolerance, age, BMI warning, and palatal CCC belong in the device/payer eligibility layer. Sex, BMI within the labeled range, PAP pressure, DISE patterns other than CCC, demographics, and research endotypes may be displayed as uncertain population-level context, but must not add points, assign a favorable/poor tier, rank HGNS above another treatment, or promise success.

### 2.4 Professional guidance on referral

**Primary guideline:** Kent D, Stanley J, Aurora RN, et al. Referral of adults with obstructive sleep apnea for surgical consultation: an AASM clinical practice guideline. *J Clin Sleep Med.* 2021;17(12):2499-2505. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.9592) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8726361/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- AASM strongly recommends discussing referral to a sleep surgeon with adults who have OSA, BMI below 40, and PAP intolerance or nonacceptance as part of a patient-oriented discussion of alternatives.
- It conditionally recommends discussion of surgical referral when PAP adherence remains inadequate because of pressure-related adverse effects.
- The guideline addresses **when to discuss surgical consultation**, not candidacy for one procedure. It does not turn BMI 40 into a universal Inspire contraindication and does not replace device labeling, DISE, or payer review.

### 2.5 Payer criteria are a distinct layer

**Primary payer source:** CMS Medicare Coverage Database, LCD L38276, Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation for Obstructive Sleep Apnea, revision effective 2026-05-14. [Current LCD](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/lcd.aspx?lcdId=38276). Accessed 2026-08-13.

The current LCD requires, among other details:

- Age at least 22.
- BMI below 35.
- PSG demonstrating AHI 15-65 within 24 months of the initial HNS consultation.
- Central plus mixed apneas below 25% of total AHI.
- Shared decision-making that documents PAP failure or intolerance despite interface and/or setting optimization.
- Absence of palatal CCC on DISE and no other anatomy expected to compromise device performance.

The LCD's PAP-intolerance documentation and AHI/BMI limits are not identical to the current FDA label. Other Medicare contractors and commercial payers may use different or changing policies.

**Precision Sleep rule:** Present this as “coverage criteria to verify” with payer name, jurisdiction/policy, version, and effective date. Never label a patient “not medically eligible” solely because the current payer will not cover treatment.

### 2.6 Objective follow-up and verification

**Primary guidance:** Caples SM, Anderson WM, Calero K, Howell M, Hashmi SD. Use of polysomnography and home sleep apnea tests for the longitudinal management of OSA in adults: an AASM clinical guidance statement. *J Clin Sleep Med.* 2021;17(6):1287-1293. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.9240) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8314660/) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33704050/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- AASM recommends follow-up PSG or HSAT to assess response to non-PAP interventions.
- The modality and timing depend on the clinical question; PSG is particularly useful for sleep-related hypoxemia/hypoventilation and may be needed for complex or unexplained findings.

**Original randomized pathway trial:** Kent D, Huyett P, Yu P, et al. Comparison of clinical pathways for hypoglossal nerve stimulation management: in-laboratory titration polysomnography vs home-based efficacy sleep testing. *J Clin Sleep Med.* 2023;19(11):1905-1912. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.10712) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620657/). Accessed 2026-08-13.

- In 60 participants, a pathway using a three-month titration PSG and a pathway using efficacy HSAT with PSG reserved for HSAT nonresponders produced equivalent six-month AHI reductions. Response rates were 63.2% vs 59.1%.
- Symptom and device-use outcomes did not meet the study's a priori equivalence criteria.
- **Limitations:** small, nonblinded pathway trial at five centers. It supports selective use of HSAT, not elimination of PSG or a single universal follow-up schedule.

**Precision Sleep rule:** Every implanted patient needs clinician-selected objective on-therapy verification. The app may propose PSG, efficacy HSAT, or staged HSAT-then-PSG based on the clinical question and prior results; it should not equate patient-reported improvement or device use with verified control of OSA.

## 3. Recommended decision architecture for the prose rationale

The main scientific-rationale document should explain the Inspire pathway as four separate questions:

1. **Is OSA objectively established with adequate, sufficiently current data?**
   - If not, obtain/review the prior study or order the clinician-selected study before candidacy is finalized.
2. **Does the patient meet current device-specific safety and indication boundaries?**
   - AHI, central/mixed burden, age, PAP failure/intolerance, BMI warning, ability to use the system, implant/MRI interactions, and other label contraindications.
3. **Has the anatomy required for unilateral Inspire been evaluated?**
   - DISE to exclude palatal CCC; other anatomy is counseling and treatment-planning context.
4. **What additional payer documentation and follow-up are required?**
   - Current policy checked separately; objective post-treatment testing is planned before treatment is called effective.

This structure prevents three recurring errors:

- Calling a referral candidate a confirmed implant candidate before the study and DISE are complete.
- Turning payer limits into universal biological or regulatory contraindications.
- Turning observational response associations into a personalized success probability.

## 4. Local-governance decisions approved by Raymond Brown, MD

Raymond Brown, MD approved all six policies on 2026-08-13. They remain local or individualized
clinical decisions where the reviewed sources do not establish a universal rule:

1. **BMI above 40 and Inspire:** retain BMI 40 as Capital ENT's boundary for automatically
   promoting an Inspire evaluation. It is a local referral guardrail, not an FDA contraindication.
2. **Short or poorly sampled home studies:** retain the four-hour warning, decision-specific REM and
   positional sampling guardrails, suppression of unsupported conclusions, and PSG escalation when
   the limitation could change diagnosis or treatment eligibility.
3. **Negative HSAT with persistent symptoms:** promote PSG as the default clinician draft. Retain
   contributor-first sequencing only as an explicit clinician-confirmed override with reassessment.
4. **Sleep-study requirements before Inspire:** do not require PSG universally when a recent,
   adequate HSAT provides the needed information and the applicable payer permits it. Verify the
   source report and current device and payer requirements.
5. **Combining DISE with another procedure:** permit a combined procedure only when the additional
   procedure has an independent indication and the clinician explicitly confirms the combined plan.
   The app must not infer the combination automatically.
6. **Testing after HGNS:** require clinician-selected objective on-therapy verification after
   activation and optimization, while leaving PSG versus HSAT and timing dependent on the clinical
   question rather than hard-coding one universal protocol.

## 5. Evidence gaps and surveillance targets

- Prospective outcomes in the expanded AHI 65-100 and BMI 32-40 Inspire populations, including the FDA-required post-approval study.
- External validation of any HGNS response model across devices, sites, scoring rules, sex, race/ethnicity, BMI strata, and payer populations.
- Better evidence separating partial from complete lateral-wall collapse and estimating the incremental value of DISE features after labeling criteria are met.
- Prospective comparison of objective follow-up pathways after HGNS, including patient-centered outcomes, residual hypoxemia, and long-term durability.
- Validated decision-specific adequacy thresholds for REM and positional sampling rather than a single technical recording-duration rule.
- Updated AASM diagnostic guidance as HSAT sensor configurations and multi-night methods evolve.
- FDA labeling and payer-policy surveillance at least before each release that changes HGNS logic or patient instructions.
