# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Capital ENT physicians and authorized clinical staff use the authenticated application during sleep-apnea evaluation, treatment planning, and follow-up. Patients do not access the clinical workspace. They may complete a patient-specific, single-use intake questionnaire on a clinic iPad or through an approved link, and they receive clinician-reviewed reports through the EHR or another approved delivery process.

## Product Purpose

Capital ENT Precision Sleep is a clinician-controlled decision-support hub for understanding why a patient has obstructive sleep apnea, matching treatment to the patient's anatomy, physiology, preferences, and prior treatment experience, and tracking response over time. Success means clinicians can move from securely collected intake and sleep-study data to an evidence-based plan and a concise patient explanation without exposing a bulk patient roster or replacing professional judgment.

## Brand Personality

Precise, trustworthy, and humane. The product should feel clinically rigorous without feeling cold, and personalized without drifting into wellness marketing or unsupported certainty.

## Anti-references

- Functional-medicine dashboards that imply unsupported root causes, causal percentages, or diagnostic certainty.
- Consumer wellness products that prioritize gamification, vague scores, or decorative data over clinical meaning.
- Surgical or device marketing that presents candidacy as a sales funnel or names a treatment before the workup supports it.
- Dense technical reports that patients cannot understand, and simplified reports that hide uncertainty or missing data.

## Design Principles

1. The explanation is the product: show patients what is contributing, what is a consequence, and what happens next.
2. Evidence before novelty: recommendations, thresholds, and wording must be traceable to clinical evidence and current labeling.
3. Make uncertainty visible: distinguish measured findings, likely contributors, risk markers, and incomplete workups.
4. Prioritize action over breadth: surface the few next steps that matter now and keep technical detail in the clinician view.
5. Clinician review is the safety boundary: patient-facing output is reviewed before it leaves the HIPAA-controlled workflow.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the clinician interface and patient reports. Reports must remain legible in print and EHR viewers, preserve meaning without color, support selectable/searchable text and screen readers where technically feasible, avoid stigmatizing weight language, and never depend on animation for comprehension.
